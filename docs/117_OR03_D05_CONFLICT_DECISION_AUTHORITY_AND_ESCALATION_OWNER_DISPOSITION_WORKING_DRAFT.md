## 1. Owner disposition

The owner conditionally approves the following conflict decision-authority and escalation principles:

1. **Technical lead:** May resolve or coordinate a technical difference only within the lead's current, evidenced competence, appointment, explicit delegation and defined scope. The technical lead cannot override a discipline authority or decide matters outside that mandate.
2. **Appointed competent technical authority:** Resolves material technical conflicts only where the authority's appointment, competence, project scope and decision delegation are evidenced and applicable to the matter.
3. **Reserved project / external authority:** Matters reserved to the owner, designer, client, statutory authority or other contractual/project authority must be referred through the applicable approved project-governance route. No reserved authority is displaced by an internal RFI role.
4. **RFI coordinator:** Performs administrative routing, recording, tracking and follow-up only. The coordinator has no technical tie-break, conflict-resolution or substitute-authority power.
5. **No assumed fallback:** The project manager, discipline lead, coordinator, committee or most senior available person does not become the default decision authority merely by title, availability, seniority, participation or administrative convenience. A substitute/acting authority requires explicit, current and evidenced appointment/delegation.
6. **Escalation is not delegation:** Referring a matter to a higher governance level does not itself transfer decision authority or establish that the recipient is competent or authorized to decide it.

## 2. Hold when authority is absent or disputed

Where the proper decision authority, appointment, competence, scope, delegation, independence or applicable external route is missing, expired, disputed, conflicted or ambiguous:
- hold the affected technical decision and dependent content/gates;
- identify the precise issue and affected scope;
- preserve all competing positions and supporting evidence;
- refer through the approved project-governance route for authority clarification or appointment evidence;
- do not infer a decision, concurrence, waiver or authority from silence, delay or availability.

The hold is scoped to the affected decision and its evidenced dependencies; it does not automatically suspend unrelated RFI content, other disciplines or the entire project.

## 3. Mandatory preservation of dissent and decision traceability

A conflict disposition must not erase, overwrite or misrepresent a discipline's original position. The record must preserve, at minimum:
- each original and subsequent technical position, author, discipline and timestamp/version;
- supporting calculations, drawings, specifications, assumptions, references and other evidence;
- the nature of the disagreement and affected interface/content;
- the classification and materiality rationale, including any challenge to that classification;
- the decision-maker's identity, appointment, competence, delegation, applicable scope and effective period;
- the decision, reasons, evidence considered, limitations, conditions and affected scope;
- dissenting or qualified opinions and whether/how they were addressed;
- escalation, referral, response and any subsequent review history.

**A decision does not erase dissent.** The decision record must distinguish the authority's adopted disposition from the continuing existence of a contrary technical opinion. It must not describe the dissenting author as having concurred unless that author expressly records concurrence.

## 4. Separate conflict resolution from independent review

The person or authority resolving a conflict is not automatically the independent reviewer of that resolution. Where DD-04 or an approved/versioned risk-and-authority matrix requires independent review, the reviewer must satisfy the separately approved competence, appointment, scope, independence and conflict-of-interest requirements.

A conflict decision cannot waive a required independent review. Equally, an independent reviewer does not become the conflict decision authority merely by reviewing or commenting on the matter, unless a separate explicit and valid delegation grants that decision power.

## 5. No majority vote or silent override

No conflict may be treated as resolved solely by:
- majority vote among contributors;
- silence, non-response or failure to object;
- the technical lead's coordination summary;
- the coordinator's administrative action;
- seniority or job title without evidenced decision authority;
- removal, suppression or rewriting of a dissenting contribution.

Any authorized resolution must identify the actual decision authority and provide a reasoned, attributable decision within its mandate. Where the matter cannot be resolved within that mandate, it remains held and is escalated.

## 6. Conditions and specialist validation

This disposition remains conditional on specialist validation by the project/process owner, appointing/project-governance authority, technical and affected discipline authorities, QA/independent assurance, Document Control/records, and contractual/regulatory authority as applicable.

Specialists must validate:
- the project's actual hierarchy and reserved decision authorities;
- appointment, delegation, acting, revocation and conflict-of-interest evidence;
- boundaries between technical lead, discipline authority, owner/designer/client and external statutory authority;
- escalation route, response ownership and hold handling when authority is absent or disputed;
- separation of conflict decision from independent review, readiness, formal issue and administrative coordination;
- the minimum durable record and retention/traceability requirements under the approved records framework.

No person is appointed, no role is granted a capability, and no project-specific authority hierarchy or escalation SLA is created by this disposition.
