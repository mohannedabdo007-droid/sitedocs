## 1. Owner disposition

The owner conditionally approves the principle of a **bounded technical-lead mandate**, exercised only within the individual's evidenced competence, current appointment and explicit delegation for the relevant project, RFI, discipline/interface and decision scope.

Within that evidenced scope, the technical lead may:
- coordinate required technical contributions and cross-discipline interfaces;
- identify missing inputs, inconsistencies, dependencies and technical gaps;
- request clarification or further explanation through the governed process;
- assess whether an issue can be handled within the lead's delegated technical mandate;
- consolidate compatible discipline positions while preserving source attribution, qualifications and dissent;
- prepare a consolidated response candidate and recommend a scoped return or escalation where the applicable rules permit;
- identify review, readiness and issue dependencies for the separately appointed authorities.

## 2. Reserved decisions and explicit limits

The technical-lead role alone does not confer authority to:
- determine, add, remove or redefine required disciplines or their technical scope;
- appoint, replace or reassign contributors, discipline leads, assessors or reviewers;
- accept, approve or override another discipline's professional conclusion;
- waive a required contribution, technical requirement, unresolved material conflict or mandatory independent review;
- decide a conflict outside the lead's evidenced competence or delegation;
- suppress, rewrite or silently replace a dissenting or qualified technical position;
- treat silence, non-response, late response or administrative completion as technical concurrence;
- confirm final readiness for issue or formally issue/adopt the response;
- alter an issued/closed response outside the separately governed post-issue route.

Any separately granted authority for a reserved action must be evidenced by a distinct, current appointment/delegation specifying the action, scope, limits and effective period. It must not be inferred from the technical-lead title.

## 3. Escalation and hold rule

A matter that exceeds, conflicts with or falls outside the lead's evidenced mandate must be referred to the properly appointed competent technical authority or approved project-governance route. Escalation does not itself transfer decision authority.

If the relevant authority, appointment, competence, scope or delegation is missing, expired, disputed, conflicted or ambiguous, the affected technical decision and dependent gate remain on hold. The RFI coordinator may route and record the matter administratively but cannot act as a technical tie-break or substitute authority.
