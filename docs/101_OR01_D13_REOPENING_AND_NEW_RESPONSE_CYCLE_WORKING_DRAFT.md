> This memorandum recommends a controlled post-issue reopening event that creates a new response cycle under the same official RFI identity. It does not authorize alteration of an issued response, create a new RFI number for the same question, appoint a technical authority, or authorize implementation/testing.

## 1. Decision question and scope

DISP-OR01-013 determines:
1. Which post-issue circumstances may justify reopening a technically closed RFI?
2. Who may refer, assess and authorize reopening?
3. What exact record and cycle identity is created?
4. How should the new cycle choose its starting stage based on the reason, scope, current authority and assignment validity?
5. Which prior records remain immutable and how are they linked?
6. How are formal revision, clarification, correction, linked new RFI, Transmittal and recipient responses distinguished?
7. How are reopening reconciled with issue history, concurrent actions, access, evidence and recovery?

This item concerns only reopening after successful formal issue and technical closure under DISP-OR01-010. It does not reopen pre-issue withdrawal/cancellation, rewrite the prior response, or decide parent/subordinate aggregation under DISP-OR01-014.

## 2. Controlling governance and protected boundaries

1. **DEC-106:** reopening is a separately authorized route; preserve prior closure and history; no default SLA, clock pause or contractual effect.
2. **DISP-OR01-010:** formal issue and technical closure are one indivisible business outcome. A successfully issued response remains an authoritative historical response; reopening cannot undo or erase it.
3. **DEC-022-A/B:** changes to controlled technical content must be classified through the formal revision policy. Reopening is not itself a revision and does not silently replace the issued snapshot.
4. **DEC-107:** RFI ID, formal revision, clarification round, response version, cycle ID, contribution ID and Transmittal ID are distinct identities. Reopening the same question does not allocate another official RFI number by default.
5. **DEC-108/103:** new formal response distribution uses the controlled Transmittal model; recipient-level delivery, access and acknowledgement remain separate. Prior recipient events remain attached to the prior Transmittal and version.
6. **DEC-109:** archive, reporting, notification, workload and retention are separate from technical lifecycle.
7. **DEC-105:** the coordinator is administrative; technical reopening authority must be evidenced for the exact project/party/RFI scope.
8. **DEC-110:** acceptance criteria require authority, negative authorization, exact-version, concurrency, evidence/history and recovery traceability. No tests are executed here.

## 3. Recommended meaning of reopening

Reopening is an authorized post-issue decision that initiates a **new, separately identifiable response cycle** for the same official RFI question or an explicitly scoped continuation of it.

It does not:
- reverse or invalidate the prior successful formal issue/technical closure transaction;
- change the prior issued response, its revision/version, evidence package, issue timestamp or closure record;
- make the prior response disappear from history or imply it was never issued;
- automatically make the prior response technically wrong, unsafe or contractually ineffective;
- create a new official RFI number for the same question;
- automatically restart all disciplines, reviews or assignments;
- grant the originator, coordinator, Document Control, recipient or reviewer an automatic right to reopen;
- imply a contractual notice, warranty, liability, deemed receipt or deemed acceptance effect.

The prior cycle remains closed and immutable. The new cycle is active under the same parent RFI identity, with an explicit predecessor link to the exact prior response and closure.

## 4. Recommended eligible grounds

Recommend allowing a reopening referral only where there is a specific, material, attributable reason requiring renewed technical assessment of the previously issued response. Candidate grounds for owner/specialist validation:

1. **Material new technical evidence:** new test results, verified site conditions, calculations, design information or source documents that could materially affect the issued answer.
2. **Identified technical error or omission:** a substantiated error, inconsistency, omitted material assumption or unresolved technical issue in the issued response.
3. **Changed technical basis or conditions:** a material change to design basis, site condition, interface, specification, code basis or approved project requirement that affects the response.
4. **Authorized post-issue direction:** a documented instruction from an authority empowered under the applicable project governance to require technical reassessment.
5. **Material conflict discovered after issue:** credible evidence that a material discipline conflict, dependency or risk was not adequately addressed before issue.

These are candidate grounds, not an exhaustive approved taxonomy. A referral must explain materiality and identify the exact affected response content, discipline, assumption, evidence and consequence. Mere dissatisfaction, disagreement without new basis, recipient silence, non-acknowledgement, failed delivery, elapsed time, workload or a desire to change wording are not sufficient grounds by default.

Where a concern is only a request to explain an unchanged answer, use the separately governed clarification route if available; do not automatically reopen the full technical cycle. Where the question is genuinely independent, create a linked new RFI. Where controlled technical content must be formally revised, apply DEC-022-A/B in addition to any required reopening decision.

## 5. Who may refer, assess and authorize

Separate the following roles:

1. **Referral:** an originator, affected project party, recipient or participant may submit an attributable post-issue concern where the project rules permit. Referral alone does not change lifecycle state or suspend the issued response.
2. **Technical assessment:** the currently appointed technical authority for the exact project/party/RFI scope determines whether the referral is technically material, whether it concerns the same RFI question and what scope requires reassessment.
3. **Reopening decision:** only a designated technical authority with a current, evidenced appointment/delegation for the exact RFI/project/party scope may authorize reopening. If a separate project governance authority must approve the decision, its requirement must be evidenced by an approved rule.
4. **Technical lead:** the appointed lead assesses affected disciplines, work packages, conflicts, prior contributions and required reassignments within the scope of appointment. The lead's recommendation is not automatically the reopening authorization unless the appointment explicitly grants that authority.
5. **Coordinator / Document Control:** performs administrative recording, routing and communication only after the authorized decision. Neither role acquires technical authority by handling the record.
6. **Originator / recipient:** cannot unilaterally reopen by submitting a clarification, substantive response, acknowledgement or objection.

If the technical authority, appointment, scope or delegation is missing, expired, disputed or unavailable, hold the referral for competent governance determination. Do not infer authority from job title, project membership, previous reviewer status or access rights.

## 6. Cutoff and prior closure integrity

Reopening is available only after a successful formal issue/technical closure event has committed. Before that event, use the applicable active lifecycle route; do not label the record reopened.

After reopening:
- prior cycle remains Closed;
- prior issued response and exact response version remain unchanged;
- prior readiness confirmation, independent review and issue evidence remain attached to the prior cycle/version;
- prior Transmittal package, recipient list, delivery/access/acknowledgement/substantive-response events remain attached to that Transmittal and version;
- prior contributions, dissent, clarifications, comments, attachments and audit events remain historically attributable;
- the new cycle has its own cycle identifier, start event, scope, authority, assignments, response candidate/version, review/readiness and issue/closure evidence.

No state is to be represented as if the prior cycle were never closed. Reopening is not an unissue, rollback or correction-in-place.

## 7. New cycle identity and lineage

Recommend:
- retain the same official parent RFI ID and official number for the same originating question;
- allocate a distinct immutable response-cycle ID and cycle sequence/version under that RFI;
- link the new cycle to the exact predecessor cycle, prior issued response version, formal revision (if any), package manifest, issue event and closure evidence;
- record the reopening decision ID, reason, scope, requester/referrer, decision authority and appointment evidence, technical assessment, affected disciplines and timestamp;
- preserve a snapshot of the prior issued response and evidence package;
- create new contribution and response candidate versions for the new cycle; do not overwrite old versions;
- allocate a new formal response version and, where required, a formal revision under DEC-022-A/B; keep the identifiers distinct;
- do not reuse prior review clearance, readiness confirmation, issue approval or response package as approval for the new cycle.

If the new work is a genuinely independent question, create a new RFI with its own official number and explicit predecessor/successor relationship. Do not overload reopening to combine different questions.

## 8. Recommended cycle-resumption and starting-stage rule

**Do not always return every reopened RFI to In Technical Assessment.** Select the new cycle's starting stage from the authorized scope and current evidenced readiness of its prerequisites. The decision must be recorded before the new cycle begins; no stage is inferred from the previous cycle's final state.

Candidate rule:

| New-cycle condition | Recommended starting point | Required guard |
|---|---|---|
| Technical question and discipline scope are defined; current technical authority, lead and required assignments remain valid and evidenced | **In Technical Assessment** | Reconfirm appointment, assignment, access, evidence and scope at cycle start; create new cycle-specific work items |
| Scope, party, disciplines, lead or assignments need to be determined/revalidated before technical work can be assigned | **Under Coordination** as a controlled routing stage | The appointed technical authority first determines scope/lead/assignments; coordinator verifies administrative completeness and executes only the delegated transition under DISP-OR01-004 |
| Essential originator information is missing and an authorized technical actor records a specific blocking dependency | Start in the valid technical assessment route, then transition to **Awaiting Originator Clarification** under DISP-OR01-005 | New-cycle clarification request, exact question/dependency, affected scope and blocker classification recorded; no automatic parent pause |
| Affected contributions can be scoped independently and only selected disciplines require renewed work | **In Technical Assessment** with only affected discipline work reopened/created | Unaffected prior contributions may be reused as historical reference only after current technical validation; no automatic acceptance or silent carry-forward |
| Reassessment changes technical content, assumptions, evidence or conflict status | Technical assessment first; later follow the approved consolidation/review/readiness gates | New candidate and evidence versions; reassess independent-review trigger and exact-version clearance under DISP-OR01-008 |
| The prior issue is being reconsidered but no valid authority, scope, evidence or assignment basis is established | **Held — no new active technical stage** pending prerequisite | Do not create a misleading active stage or imply authority/acceptance |

Additional controls:
1. Reopening must not jump directly to Under Consolidation, Under Independent Review, Ready for Issue or Closed.
2. Prior completed contributions may be considered as inputs, but must be explicitly revalidated for current relevance, scope and version by the currently appointed technical authority/lead. They do not automatically satisfy new-cycle required-contribution gates.
3. Prior independent review clearance and readiness confirmation never transfer to a new response candidate/version.
4. Any mandatory independent review is re-triggered/reassessed against the current approved matrix and exact new candidate version.
5. New cycle must satisfy DISP-OR01-005 through DISP-OR01-009 as applicable, then DISP-OR01-010 for formal issue/technical closure.
6. If assignments or scope changed, apply the two-key routing and complete-assignment gate under DISP-OR01-004; do not infer old appointments remain current.
7. The starting-stage mapping is a proposed decision rule, not approval of new lifecycle labels or an implementation-ready state machine.

## 9. Reassessment of contributions, conflicts and review

At reopening, the technical authority/lead must record:
- which prior contributions remain relevant and why;
- which contributions require targeted reassessment, replacement or new versions;
- which disciplines are affected and which are unaffected;
- whether new or changed evidence affects assumptions, interfaces, design basis or risk;
- whether prior conflict/dissent resolution remains valid;
- whether the approved independent-review matrix triggers review for the new candidate;
- whether prior reviewer appointment remains valid for the new scope and whether independence/conflict checks must be repeated;
- what exact evidence and candidate manifest are in scope for the new cycle.

Do not copy prior completion status into new work without an attributable revalidation decision. Preserve prior contribution versions and dissent. No majority vote or silence is implied as concurrence.

## 10. Relationship to clarification, revision, correction and linked new RFI

- **Clarification:** asks for explanation or additional detail without changing controlled technical content, under the separately governed clarification route. It does not silently edit an issued response.
- **Formal revision:** a controlled change to technical content under DEC-022-A/B. Reopening may provide the lifecycle context for reassessment, but formal revision classification and identity remain separate.
- **Correction:** a documented correction route may be required for an error; it must not overwrite the prior issue. Whether a specific post-issue correction requires reopening and/or formal revision is subject to the approved correction policy and competent authority.
- **Linked new RFI:** a genuinely independent question gets its own official RFI identity and explicit relationship to the predecessor; do not reuse the old number for a different question.
- **Reopening:** starts a new response cycle for the same RFI question while preserving the previous issued response and closure.

The exact boundary between post-issue clarification, correction and reopening must be reconciled with the applicable approved DEC-022-A/B rules and specialist advice before operational use.

## 11. Transmittal, delivery, access and recipient response

A new formal response from the new cycle must use the controlled formal distribution route under DEC-108:
- create a new immutable response package and Transmittal identity for the new issue;
- preserve the prior Transmittal, recipient snapshot, delivery attempts, access events, acknowledgements and substantive responses;
- do not transfer acknowledgements from the prior response/version to the new response;
- resolve required/optional recipient acknowledgement independently for each Transmittal under DEC-103/108;
- internal authorized access to the RFI record remains distinct from formal external issue/distribution;
- notification or recipient silence cannot authorize reopening, approve the new response or close the new cycle.

Reopening alone does not automatically issue a notice, create a contractual notice or establish deemed receipt.

## 12. Required reopening decision and cycle record

Minimum evidence:
- stable reopening request/referral ID and version;
- official RFI ID/number and exact prior closed cycle ID;
- exact prior response version, formal revision, package manifest, issue event, closure evidence and Transmittal references;
- reason code and reasoned materiality statement;
- exact affected question, technical content, assumptions, evidence, disciplines and scope;
- referrer identity, party context and permission evidence;
- appointed technical authority and delegation/appointment instrument, scope and validity;
- technical assessment and authorized reopening decision;
- appointed technical lead and affected discipline/assignment decisions;
- new cycle ID/sequence and predecessor linkage;
- starting-stage rationale and required gates;
- prior contributions/reviews/evidence reused as references and explicit revalidation decisions;
- new contribution/response version lineage;
- date/time, actor, policy version, event correlation and audit references.

## 13. Concurrency, idempotency and failure handling

1. Reopening is allowed only against a successfully committed Closed cycle and exact prior issue/closure references.
2. Serialize reopening authorization and new-cycle creation against duplicate reopening attempts, corrections, clarifications, linked-new-RFI creation and any other post-issue action affecting the same response.
3. A repeated request for the same authorized reopening decision must resolve to one logical new cycle; no duplicate active cycles.
4. If the create operation times out after success, reconcile authoritative cycle/event history before retry.
5. Revalidate authority, appointment, scope, prior closed cycle, evidence, and current policy at the consequential commit.
6. If a stale or superseded prior cycle is referenced, hold and require re-evaluation.
7. If two reopening requests overlap, require an authorized decision on whether they belong to one cycle or distinct scoped cycles; do not merge them automatically.
8. If a genuinely independent question is identified, route to linked new RFI rather than a second reopening cycle for a different question.
9. Partial failure must not expose a new cycle without its predecessor, authority, scope and start-stage record.
10. No database transaction, locking, schema, idempotency or recovery implementation is authorized here.

## 14. Proposed acceptance criteria for later test design

Design-only criteria; no tests have been executed:
1. A participant may refer a post-issue concern but cannot reopen without current scoped technical authority.
2. Reopening is denied if no valid material ground, exact prior response or authority evidence exists.
3. Reopening cannot change the prior issued response, issue event, closure record or prior Transmittal/recipient history.
4. A valid reopening creates one distinct new cycle under the same official RFI identity with exact predecessor linkage.
5. A genuinely independent question creates a linked new RFI with a new official number, not a reopened cycle.
6. The starting stage is selected from documented scope/authority/assignment prerequisites; it is not hard-coded to one universal stage.
7. A cycle requiring new assignments cannot bypass the two-key routing and complete-assignment gate.
8. A reopened cycle cannot jump directly to Under Consolidation, Under Independent Review, Ready for Issue or Closed.
9. Prior contributions may be reused only as traceable inputs after explicit current-cycle revalidation; prior review/readiness clearance never transfers automatically.
10. Mandatory review is reassessed against the current approved matrix and exact candidate version.
11. Formal revision classification remains distinct and follows DEC-022-A/B.
12. New formal response distribution uses a new Transmittal; prior recipient acknowledgements do not satisfy new-cycle requirements.
13. Duplicate/concurrent reopening attempts do not create duplicate active cycles.
14. Missing/expired/disputed authority, scope, evidence, appointment or predecessor linkage holds the action.
15. Unauthorized direct/API attempts fail even if UI controls are hidden or disabled.
16. No notification, delivery, access, acknowledgement, silence or archive event independently reopens the RFI.

Blocking defects include alteration/deletion of prior issued content, duplicate active cycles, inherited review/readiness clearance, wrong RFI identity/number reuse, unauthorized reopening, missing predecessor lineage, bypass of required assignment/review gates, or transfer of prior recipient acknowledgement to a new response.

## 16. Conditions and interim controls

Until owner disposition and specialist validation:
- no operational reopening is authorized by this memorandum;
- preserve prior issued response, exact package, issue and closure records without modification;

## 17. Technical recommendation summary

Adopt reopening as a controlled post-issue event that creates one new response cycle under the same official RFI identity, while preserving the prior issued response, closure and Transmittal history unchanged. A currently appointed technical authority must authorize reopening on a reasoned, material, evidence-based case. The new cycle's starting stage must be selected from its current scope, authority, assignment and clarification prerequisites, not forced universally to Technical Assessment. Prior contributions may inform the new cycle only after explicit revalidation; prior independent-review clearance and readiness never transfer. Formal revision, clarification, correction, linked new RFI, new Transmittal and recipient events remain separate. No direct jump to later lifecycle gates or duplicate active cycles is permitted.
